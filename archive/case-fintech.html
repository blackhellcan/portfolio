Mutual funds that first-time investors trust enough to start

Designing a mutual fund module inside an existing stock-trading app, for people who have never invested as much as for seasoned traders. The project stopped during design review, before development, when the client paused all outside work.

The problem

Most people who could invest in mutual funds don’t, because they don’t understand them or don’t trust them. The client wanted a metrics-driven way to invest in and track mutual funds, built into its existing stock-broking app.

It had to work for traditional and first-time investors, explain risk and past performance honestly, including bad years, and still feel quick for experienced investors who already trade stocks in the same app.

My role

  • Ran the alignment workshop to agree challenges, aspirations, focus areas and guiding principles with the client.
  • Benchmarked Groww, Coin by Zerodha and Paytm Money across the five phases of investing.
  • Wrote the job stories and user-intent document that set feature requirements.
  • Prioritised features with a value–effort mapping session, then designed the IA, wireflows and wireframes.
  • Led a two-person design team.

What we learned

New investors want to start but fear losing money.

“Everybody tells me that you should start investing early. I don’t have lumpsum to invest right now, but I want to start. I fear if I make wrong investment decision I might lose my money.” (proto-persona, first-time investor)

→

Let people look around before signing up, and explain each fund in plain terms before asking for money.

Savvy investors want depth, fast.

They compare holdings, returns, risk and fund managers before deciding, and switch between charts and numbers.

→

Fund detail pages carry the full breakdown, one tap away from the simple view.

Control matters as much as starting.

“When I plan to stop my systematic investment, I want to do so using a kill switch, so that I don’t have to find each investment separately.” (job story)

→

SIPs can be paused or stopped from one place.

Key decisions

Decision 1

Guest access and a guided first look for new investors

Chose
Guest login without Aadhaar, PAN or bank details, an onboarding tour that explains strategy, budget and how the app works, and a “SIP with ₹100” entry point among the top picks.
Why
Fear of a wrong decision stopped first-timers before they started. Letting them look around first lowers the cost of curiosity.
Trade-off
Guests still had to complete KYC before their first investment, so the hurdle moved rather than disappeared.
Decision 2

Ship the essentials before the novel features

Options
Six hygiene features (guest login, onboarding tour, fund breakdowns, payment and SIP tracking, personalised alerts, chart or number views) and four unique ones (sharing with family and friends, linked news, voice help, reviews from real investors).
Chose
Value–effort mapping put the hygiene set in the first release; the unique features were planned for later.
Why
Trust comes from the basics working well. Novel features don’t help if tracking a SIP is confusing.
Trade-off
The features that would set the app apart from Groww and Coin came later.
Decision 3

One cart for SIPs and lump sums

Chose
Investors add funds to a cart, set SIP dates and amounts or a one-time amount for each, and see a clear split between SIP and lump-sum payments before paying.
Why
From the user-intent document: investors wanted to “make a single purchase and save time”, and to see “a comprehensive understanding of the payment breakdown”.
Trade-off
One extra step for someone buying a single fund, softened by an “Invest now” button beside “Add to cart”.

The solution

Each screen answers a job story. The fund detail serves the investor deciding go or no-go on a fund. The SIP calculator serves “how much should I invest every month?”. The portfolio serves tracking, and the mandate and order screens make the money side transparent.

Two rounds of review

Validation and impact

  • Did not launch. Leadership changed on the client side, and the new team put all external engagements on hold. The project stopped during design review, before development.
  • Delivered before the pause: workshop outcomes, competitor benchmarks, job stories, a prioritised feature set, and two reviewed rounds of the core screens: dashboard, fund detail, autopay mandate and order status, plus hi-fi mockups.

Reflection

I leaned on reviews instead of leading. It was one of my first projects as a lead, and I went to the product and project managers for review and feedback at every stage. With what I know now, I would set up a clear process from the start: agreed milestones, review points and decision owners, so the team could move faster with fewer check-ins.
